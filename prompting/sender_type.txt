Ports and sockets\nA port number helps an operating system direct incoming network traffic to the correct application. A socket⁶ is an operating system endpoint applications use to send & receive data over a network.\nTCP connections are identified by four values:\nsource IP address,\nsource port,\ndestination IP address,\ndestination port.\nThese form a unique connection identifier called a four-tuple.\nServers listen on well-known ports, such as 443 for HTTPS traffic. On many Linux systems, the default range is 32768-60999. i.e., about 28,000 ports.\nAnalogy\nAn apartment building with one street address.\nThe building address identifies the machine, while the apartment number identifies where a message should go. This helps avoid port exhaustion & reduces the overhead of establishing new connections.\n10. VPN\nA Virtual Private Network (VPN) creates an encrypted tunnel between devices/networks over a public network, such as the internet.\nIt protects data in transit & allows users to access private network resources remotely.\nHere’s how it works:\nTunnel establishment: Your device establishes a secure connection with a VPN gateway.\nEncapsulation: VPN wraps outgoing packets inside new packets for transmission across the internet.\nEncryption: VPN encrypts protected traffic so intermediaries cannot read its contents.\nTransmission: The encrypted packets travel across the public network to the VPN gateway.\nDecapsulation: Gateway decrypts & unwraps the packets, then forwards them to their destination.\nHere are two common VPN configurations:\nRemote-access VPN: Connects individual devices to a private network.\nSite-to-site VPN: Connects entire networks, such as two office locations.\nWireGuard & IPsec are widely used VPN technologies.\nWireGuard offers a relatively simple design with modern cryptography, while IPsec supports a broader range of configurations and legacy infrastructure.\nAnalogy\nImagine a secure tunnel connecting two buildings through a public city.\nPeople outside see where the tunnel starts and ends, but they cannot see what travels inside.\nTradeoff\nVPNs add encryption & encapsulation overhead.\nThis consumes processing resources & reduce space for packet payloads.\nPlus, additional routing through a VPN gateway increases latency. Incorrect Maximum Transmission Unit (MTU) settings also cause fragmentation or packet drops.\nIf the VPN connection fails, some configurations may allow traffic to bypass the tunnel. A kill switch, strict routing, and firewall rules prevent unintended traffic leaks.\nWhy it matters\nVPNs help to securely access private databases, internal services & cloud networks.\nUse WireGuard for straightforward VPN deployments or IPsec when interoperability with existing enterprise networking equipment matters.\n§\nFinal Words\nRemember Jung, whose tiny VPC change took down an entire app?\nNow you understand the 10 networking concepts he wished he’d known.\nBut we’re only getting started…\nSee you in Part 2!\nIf you find this newsletter valuable, share it with a friend, and subscribe if you haven’t already.
